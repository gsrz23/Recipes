Seafood Paella

Prep: 25 mins  |  Cook: 40 mins  |  Servings: 6  |  Pan: 15-inch Paella Pan

Ingredients

The Broth

  • 5 cups clam or seafood broth (plus extra on standby)
  • 1 tsp saffron threads

The Seafood

  • 1 ½ lbs firm-fleshed white fish (monkfish, cod, or halibut), cut into bite-sized pieces
  • 12 large shrimp, shell-on
  • 1 dozen mussels, scrubbed and debearded
  • 1 dozen small clams (like littlenecks), scrubbed
  • Spanish sea salt, to taste

The Herb Paste (Picada)

  • 8 cloves garlic, minced
  • 2 tbsp fresh flat-leaf parsley, minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves
  • 2 tsp sweet smoked paprika (pimentón dulce)
  • ⅛ tsp Spanish sea salt
  • 1–2 tsp water (as needed)

The Base (Sofrito) & Rice

  • 8 tbsp (½ cup) extra-virgin olive oil, divided
  • 1 medium yellow onion, finely chopped
  • 6 scallions, chopped
  • 1 red bell pepper, finely chopped
  • 1 large ripe tomato, chopped
  • 2 cups Bomba or Calasparra paella rice

To Serve

  • Lemon wedges
  • Alioli (garlic mayonnaise)

Preparation

1. Prep the Broth, Seafood, and Herb Paste

  1. Infuse the broth: Pour the broth into a saucepan and bring to a gentle simmer over medium-low heat. Stir in the saffron threads and keep warm on the back burner.
  2. Season the seafood: Pat the fish pieces and shrimp dry with paper towels. Sprinkle generously with sea salt and let sit at room temperature for 10 minutes. Keep the mussels and clams chilled until needed.
  3. Make the herb paste: Using a mortar and pestle, mash the garlic, parsley, thyme, and ⅛ tsp salt into a rough paste. Stir in the smoked paprika, adding a splash of water if needed to loosen it into a spreadable consistency. Set aside.

2. Sear the Fish & Build the Sofrito

  1. Sear the fish: Heat 6 tbsp of olive oil in a 15-inch paella pan over medium-high heat. Add the fish pieces and quickly sear for 1–2 minutes just to get light color on the outside without cooking through. Transfer the fish to a warm plate and set aside.
  2. Sauté the aromatics: Add the remaining 2 tbsp of olive oil to the pan. Toss in the onion, scallions, and red bell pepper. Cook for 4–5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until softened.
  3. Add the tomato: Increase the heat to high and add the chopped tomato. Cook for 2–5 minutes, stirring frequently, until the liquid evaporates and the mixture thickens into a jammy, sauce-like base.

3. Cook the Rice

  1. Add broth and rice: Carefully pour the hot saffron broth into the pan and bring to a rolling boil. Sprinkle the rice evenly across the pan.
  2. Initial boil: Boil vigorously for 3 minutes, gently stirring the rice and rotating the pan over the burner so it heats evenly.
  3. Add flavor and fish: Gently nestle the seared fish pieces into the pan (leave the shrimp out for now). Stir in the garlic-herb paste until well distributed. Taste the broth for salt.
Crucial Rule: Do not stir the rice again after this point.
  1. Simmer: Lower the heat to medium-low. Simmer gently for 10 minutes until the rice is no longer soupy, though plenty of liquid should still remain to finish cooking the grains. Add a splash of hot water or extra broth if the pan looks dry too quickly.

4. Steam the Shellfish & Rest

  1. Arrange the shellfish: Press the shrimp, clams, and mussels into the top layer of the rice. Place the clams and mussels with their hinged edges down so they open facing upward, trapping their flavorful juices.
  2. Finish cooking: Continue cooking, uncovered, over low heat for 15–20 minutes until the rice is tender, the liquid is absorbed, and the clams and mussels have opened. Discard any shellfish that remain tightly closed.
For Socarrat (Optional): During the final 1–2 minutes, turn the heat up to medium-high until you hear a faint crackling sound and smell toasted rice at the bottom of the pan.
  1. Rest and serve: Remove the pan from the heat, cover loosely with aluminum foil, and let it rest for 10 minutes. Serve straight from the pan with lemon wedges and alioli on the side.
